## Top-line risk assessment

**The core change is correct, well-tested at its centre, and materially improves
the codebase.** `FromReply`'s two design properties — recognition by discriminator
rather than by decodability, and keeping an unmodellable failure untyped — are
the right calls, and the five fail-opens fixed in the later commits are real
defects that were each reproduced before being changed. The oplog-transformer
one was silently discarding migration operations.

**What holds it back from "merge as-is" is not the helper — it is the blast
radius of the 21 call-site conversions, which is wider than the PR body states.**
Three specific gaps:

1. **The PR's own "read one, you've read them all" claim is false.** The five
   "mechanical" sites comprise three distinct pre-change shapes and three distinct
   consumption shapes. One of them — `broadcast-worker/parent_fetcher.go` — turns
   a silent degraded fan-out into no fan-out plus a full `MaxDeliver` NAK budget
   under version skew. That is a deliberate and defensible trade, but it is not
   mechanical and it is not mentioned.
2. **Two behaviour regressions ride along unflagged**: `room-service`'s
   `not_room_member` remap is now gated behind a valid code (a legacy peer's 403
   becomes a 500), and `tools/clientsim`'s unknown-code replies flip from terminal
   to retried-forever, which the surrounding code explicitly warns must not happen.
3. **TDD coverage is uneven.** The five headline fixes each got a red test. The
   sixteen other converted sites got none, including three branches in
   `botplatform-service` that exist specifically to prevent a panic, and the new
   null-rejection logic in `user-service/roomclient`, which sits in a package at
   **0% unit coverage** behind an integration tag that cannot run without Docker.

None of this is a reason to abandon the approach. All of it is a reason to land
the missing tests and either fix or explicitly document the three behaviour
changes before merge.

**Also worth noting for the author's confidence in the guards:** one claim raised
during review — that the semgrep rule's `"/pkg/errcode/"` path exclude is
malformed and would break the blocking `sast` gate — was checked directly against
the real scanner and **does not hold**; the full-tree scan is clean. Two *other*
bypasses of the guard pair were found and empirically confirmed (§6).
